The 30-second version

Pigtail's synthesis of the evidence below. Evidence level: Strong inference.

PocketBase is an MIT-licensed Go backend that ships as a single executable with an embedded SQLite database, realtime subscriptions, auth and an admin dashboard. It is positioned as a lightweight alternative to Firebase or Supabase. The repository was created on July 5, 2022. The first release and a Show HN post came out on July 7, followed by a Product Hunt launch on July 8. By October 2026 it had about 61,000 stars, 292 releases and 45 contributors. Star growth since launch has come in a series of short bursts that line up in time with large Hacker News posts and GitHub trending placements, with steadier growth between them.

Growth mechanism

The evidence points to a loop driven by recurring exposure rather than one launch. People other than the maintainer kept reposting PocketBase to Hacker News, often reusing the '1 file' tagline. Two reposts, in January 2024 and November 2025, drew more points than the original Show HN. Over 214 days the repo also returned to GitHub trending, including top-5 placements across all languages in December 2023, April 2025 and July 2025. Star bursts happened around the same time as several of these placements. Separately, a third-party ecosystem grew alongside the project: unofficial hosting (PocketHost), community clients in several languages, and deployment guides. The permissive license allows this kind of outside work. The claims do not measure how much each part feeds the others.

Origin

PocketBase started in 2022 as an open source Go project with Bulgaria listed as its country of origin. It is listed as a hobby project on dbdb.io. The public repository was created on July 5, 2022, and v0.1.0 was published two days later. The claims do not say why it was built.

What PocketBase did differently

Higher-level interpretation, separate from the factual record above.

One reading is that PocketBase competed on simplicity of deployment. It packages the API server, admin dashboard and database into one file and states that constraint plainly in its tagline. It also shipped very quickly after launch: 15 releases between July 7 and July 21, 2022. It keeps a tight scope through a maintainer roadmap, asks contributors to discuss new-feature PRs first, and makes no backward-compatibility guarantee before v1. The single-file design has a known trade-off: it cannot scale horizontally, and one source suggests it suits projects with up to around 10,000 users.
