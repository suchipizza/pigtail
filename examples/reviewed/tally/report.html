The 30-second version

Pigtail's synthesis of the evidence below. Evidence level: Stated in sources.

Tally is a bootstrapped form builder from Ghent, Belgium. It gives away unlimited forms and submissions for free, and charges for a single Pro tier, later joined by a Business tier. Free forms carry a 'Made with Tally' badge, so the people who fill out forms see the product. For about five years growth came mainly from that badge, word of mouth and organic search, with little paid spend. Around 2025–2026 AI assistants such as ChatGPT became its largest acquisition channel. Tally reported over $6M ARR and 2.5 million users in September 2026 without raising VC money.

Growth mechanism

The free plan sets the cycle in motion. Because forms and submissions are unlimited at no cost, many people publish forms. Each free form shows a 'Made with Tally' badge to its respondents, and Tally says most people discover it by filling out a form. Some respondents sign up and create forms of their own. Tally has put the share of free users who upgrade to Pro at about 1–3%, and that revenue funds the company. Word of mouth, personal support, comparison pages and organic search add to this loop. Tally attributes its later AI referrals to years of presence in forums, Reddit and user-generated content, which suggests the earlier organic activity fed the newer channel.

Origin and first users

Why it was built and how the earliest users were found, as far as public sources show.
First users

The first users were makers, indie hackers, no-coders and startup founders. A co-founder manually messaged hundreds of people, by one account over 1,000, who had engaged with similar products on Product Hunt and Twitter. The few who replied became the first users and gave feedback. Everyone they talked to was invited into a Slack channel, which grew to about 400 people by late 2020. The founders also answered questions in no-code communities, Reddit and Indie Hackers, offered instant support and shipped features weekly. Tally had about 300 users after its first two to three months and ended 2020 with about 1,500. The March 2021 Product Hunt launch roughly doubled that to 3,000.

Reusable tactics

Concrete actions another team could understand and adapt, with where and when this project used them.

Unlimited free tier as wedge

Give away unlimited forms and responses and nearly all features. Charge only for a single Pro tier.

Mechanism: It removes the paywall frustration users feel with incumbents and differentiates in a crowded market. Wide free usage feeds distribution.

Sep 2020: Offered unlimited forms and responses with 99% of features free, and Pro at $29/month.

Needs: Low marginal cost per free user

Branded badge on free output

Put a 'Made with Tally' badge on free users' forms. Removing it is a paid feature.

Mechanism: Every form respondent sees the product, so usage turns into acquisition.

Since day one: Free forms show the badge; branding removal is part of Pro. Tally experimented with the badge design. The badge brings about 10% of new users per one source and is called the most important lead source in another.

Needs: Product output is seen by third parties

Mechanism: These are pre-qualified early adopters, and the few who reply become first users and feedback sources.

How the strategy changed

Growth approach over time (strategy phases), shown separately from the business's situation (company stages).
Strategy phases
  1. 2020 – early 2021Founder-led outreach and community feedback

    The founders cold-messaged hundreds of makers found on Product Hunt and Twitter. They also joined Slack groups, Reddit and forums to ask for feedback, and invited early users into a Slack channel. They offered instant support and shipped weekly.

  2. 2021 – early 2025Free-product-led organic growth

    Growth came from a generous free tier, the 'Made with Tally' badge, word of mouth, SEO and comparison pages, with no paid spend. From early 2023 to early 2025 Tally mostly shipped free features.

  3. 2021Product Hunt launch and building in public

    Tally launched on Product Hunt in March 2021. It shared its journey on Twitter, Indie Hackers and Reddit, which it calls its main marketing channel. It also leaned on no-code communities, newsletters and podcasts.

  4. Sept 2023Tally 2.0 relaunch

    Tally relaunched as Tally 2.0 on Product Hunt, promoted alongside social media and its newsletter.

  5. 2024–2025Paid experiments and conversion focus

    In 2024 Tally tested YouTube influencer partnerships. In 2025 it ran its first search ads and launched a referral program. It also shifted to shipping highly requested Pro and customization features.

  6. 2025–2026AI search and AI-assistant distribution

    Tally invested in answer-engine and search optimization (AEO/SEO) and tracked citations by large language models (LLMs). It released an MCP server, public API and AI connectors. AI search became its biggest acquisition channel.

  7. 2026Product quality over revenue targets

    For 2026 Tally dropped revenue goals in favor of NPS and user feedback. It rules out enterprise plans, sales calls, partnerships and big launches.

Company stages
  1. Oct 2020 – late 2022Two-founder bootstrapped, customer-funded business

    Tally Pro was introduced in October 2020. The founders say Tally has been cash-flow positive from the start. For about two years the co-founders ran it alone as a profitable, bootstrapped business funded by subscriptions. MRR rose from $64 in October 2020 to $30K in October 2022.

  2. 2023–2024Small bootstrapped team past $100K MRR

    In 2023 Tally made its first hires, starting with customer success. It hired engineers in 2023 and later downscaled that team. By the Tally 2.0 launch in September 2023 it was a team of five. It reached $100K MRR in February 2024; that source gives a team of 3 and over 250,000 users. Tally moved into its first office in 2024.

  3. 2025–2026Multi-million ARR, still bootstrapped

    Sources give $2M ARR in February 2025 ($175K MRR) and March 2025 (secondary); both dates are kept. Tally reached $3M ARR in June 2025, $4M ARR in October 2025, $5M ARR in April 2026 and $6M ARR with 2.5 million users in September 2026. It never raised VC and has no external shareholders. Reported team sizes vary across sources: 10, 11, and 4 full-time plus 4 or 5 part-time.

What Tally Forms did differently

Higher-level interpretation, separate from the factual record above.

In a market already held by Google Forms, Typeform and Jotform, Tally competed mainly on price structure rather than features. It gave away unlimited forms, responses and about 99% of features. It paired this with a Notion-style editor where building a form works like typing in a document. It also let people build a form before creating an account. It kept a single Pro tier, built in public, and kept the team small for years. Later, it treated AI assistants as a distribution surface: it shipped an MCP server, a public API and ChatGPT and Claude connectors, and it tracked how often LLMs cited it.
