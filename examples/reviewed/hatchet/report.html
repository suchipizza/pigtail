The 30-second version

Pigtail's synthesis of the evidence below. Evidence level: Strong inference.

Hatchet is an MIT-licensed orchestration engine built on Postgres, with a paid hosted version called Hatchet Cloud. It started in Y Combinator's Winter 2024 batch, and its repository has grown to about 8,000 stars. The biggest jumps in stars came around the same time as the founder's Hacker News posts. Those included Show HN and Launch HN posts and deep technical posts about Postgres. The open-source README points new users to the cloud product, and the company reports that its cloud revenue grew 9x in 2025.

Growth mechanism

A plausible loop shows up in the evidence. Hatchet co-founder Alexander Belanger's technical posts and launches on Hacker News bring developers to the open-source repo. The README sends even would-be self-hosters to Hatchet Cloud, where the company reports significant revenue. Feedback from Discord and shared Slack channels shapes what gets built next. For example, fanout workflows were the most common request after the Show HN, so Hatchet launched child workflows. Each new feature or engineering lesson becomes another post, and star spikes followed several of these posts. The data shows timing only, so whether the posts caused the growth is not established.

Origin and first users

Why it was built and how the earliest users were found, as far as public sources show.
Origin

Alexander Belanger was previously CTO at Porter. There he spent several years building an in-house orchestration system and wrote three versions of its provisioner. He later researched durable execution platforms and built a Terraform automation project on Temporal, which he abandoned. He then introduced Temporal at another company before starting Hatchet. Hatchet was co-founded with Gabe Ruttner, the former CTO of Clearmix. The founders describe it as built out of frustration with existing tools and a belief that Postgres is the right base for a task queue. It entered YC W24 with just an idea, and the repository was created on December 15, 2023.

First users

The first users appear to have been developers reached through Hacker News during the YC batch. A January 2024 Show HN got little response (7 points). A March 8, 2024 Show HN for an 'open-source distributed task queue' drew 578 points. That same day the repository gained 915 stars, within a 21-day run of 2,076 stars. At that point the focus was on open source, and the cloud was invite-only. Hatchet pitched itself as an alternative to Celery, BullMQ and Sidekiq, and the founders say they migrated a number of Celery users. The sources do not identify specific early customers by name.

How the strategy changed

Growth approach over time (strategy phases), shown separately from the business's situation (company stages).
Strategy phases
  1. Jan–Jun 2024Hacker News launches during YC

    Early Show HN (Jan 2024) then a larger Show HN (Mar 2024) with focus on open source, followed by Launch HN for self-serve cloud in June 2024.

  2. 2024–2025Celery-alternative positioning and Postgres technical content

    Positioned as Postgres-backed alternative to Celery/BullMQ/Sidekiq; published Celery critique, Postgres engineering posts, Python and FastAPI migration content.

  3. Dec 12, 2025Repositioning toward AI agents and durable execution

    Moved to describing itself as orchestration for AI agents, background tasks and durable workflows, and as Temporal/DBOS replacement; states AI agent adoption drives growth.

  4. 2026Developer-experience and enterprise feature cadence

    In 2026 shipped CLI, TUI, VSCode extension, OpenTelemetry, webhooks, enterprise/multi-team features, idempotency keys, concurrency features and pay-as-you-go pricing.

Company stages
  1. Dec 2023 – Jun 2024YC-backed idea-stage open-source project

    Hatchet started in YC W24 with just an idea; repo created Dec 2023; fully open source with an invite-only cloud offering at launch.

  2. Jun 2024 onwardOpen-source with self-serve cloud revenue

    Self-serve Hatchet Cloud launched in June 2024. Hatchet is MIT licensed with no enterprise edition and says its cloud version generates significant revenue.

  3. 2025Scaling cloud business with small team

    In 2025, Hatchet says, cloud revenue grew 9x, founding engineers joined and the v1 rewrite shipped. Its infrastructure later ran on more than 25 shards in 3 regions, and pay-as-you-go pricing was announced in September 2026.

What Hatchet did differently

Higher-level interpretation, separate from the factual record above.

Hatchet chose Postgres as its only required dependency, so it can run on any Postgres provider. It built its own partitioning instead of relying on extensions. It also shipped a full platform with observability, alerting, replays and logging, rather than a library. The whole product is MIT licensed with no enterprise edition, and revenue comes from the hosted cloud. Its marketing leaned on detailed engineering writing about running Postgres at scale more than on product announcements. This reading is interpretive.

Reversals and constraints

Documented changes of course and limits that shaped growth.
Decided against terminal coding-agent product lineMay 2025 · Hatchet considered a new product line around terminal-based coding agents but decided against it.
New pay-as-you-go pricingSep 29, 2026 · Hatchet announced new pay-as-you-go pricing.
Postgres scaling limitsHatchet's engineering blog describes the Postgres limits it hit while scaling: its single runs table bloated at about 200 million rows; it saw CPU spikes and lock contention; jsonb payloads took 50% to 90% of database storage; and after the v1 rollout, a production incident made queries on partitioned tables up to 10x slower.
Open-source portability requirementsMust run on any Postgres provider and cannot assume poolers, so built own partitioning and in-memory pooling.
Documentation and support burdenHatchet wrote that its documentation was poor around the start of 2025, with SDK examples drifting out of sync, and that it tried maintaining the docs with LLMs but stopped because the generated code was unreliable. It also says blocked event loops are the most common problem it sees when supporting users.
Shipping and reliability bottlenecksIn its “andon cord” post, Hatchet described pausing work for a week to rewrite its deployment process after releases piled up, and for two weeks to instrument critical-path endpoints after customers alerted it to unexpected latency “before we were aware of it”. Hatchet also wrote that it did not ship a new latest version in fall 2025 while cleaning up after removing v0.
